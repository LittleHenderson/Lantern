How to use Lantern

Lantern reads your app's code, shows every path a visitor can take through it, and tells you in plain words what to fix before you ship. This page walks you through it, one task at a time.

    The 60-second tour

    From a link to a fix, the way most people use Lantern the first time.

    1Sign in

    Your checks are tied to your account, so only you can open them.

    1. Open Lantern and click Sign in at the top right.
    2. Click Continue with GitHub or Continue with Google. A new tab opens on that site.
    3. Approve Lantern there. The tab tells you when you're in; go back to the Lantern tab, which now shows your account name.
    Good to knowLantern keeps your account name and nothing else: no email and no password. You type your password on GitHub or Google, never on Lantern.

    2Check your first app

    Paste a link to your app's code, or try one of the sample apps.

    1. Paste your app's link into the box that says "Paste a link, like https://github.com/you/app".
    2. Choose Map and findings only to keep no code with your results, or choose Include code snippets (and tick the box that appears) to keep short snippets in the report.
    3. Click Check my app. Lantern starts the check straight away.
    4. No app yet? Under "Try a sample app", click Check this sample on any card. Each sample has problems planted on purpose.
    Good to knowLantern copies your app into a sealed box, reads it without installing or running anything, and deletes the copy when the check ends. Only the results stay.

    3Check a private app or files on your computer

    No public link? Upload the folder instead.

    1. Click Upload… next to the link box.
    2. Click Choose a folder and pick your app's folder, or click Choose a .zip file.
    3. Or skip the button: drop a folder or a .zip straight onto the link box.
    4. Wait a moment: the check starts by itself as soon as the upload finishes.
    Good to knowFolders such as node_modules, .git, build output and virtual environments are left out of an upload. The screen shows the size limit. When the site has GitHub connection switched on, a Connect private repositories button also appears under the link box, so Lantern can read a private repository you choose; when it is not switched on, the button is not shown.

    4Follow a check and find your runs

    Watch each step as it happens, then come back to any check later.

    1. While a check runs, watch the four steps: "Copy the repository", "Check dependency versions", "Map the code and run the checks" and "Save your results", with a live log below.
    2. Every check is listed under "Your runs" on the home screen. Click My runs at the top to get back there from anywhere.
    3. On a run's card, click Show me what to fix to open it, or click Re-check to check the latest code again.
    4. If a check stopped, its card says so; click Try again.
    Good to knowThe "Where in your app" boxes on a card (Browser, Frontend, Server, Data, or Screen and App for phone apps) light up where the problems are.

    5Read what to fix

    Every problem Lantern found, most serious first, in plain words.

    1. In an open run, click What to fix.
    2. Read the top card first. Each card says what is wrong, how serious it is, and which page or route a visitor would use to reach it.
    3. Click See it happen: a scenario for a short story of one person running into the problem.
    4. Click Show the lines to see where in the code it is, or click Open in Atlas to see it on the map.
    Good to knowFindings come from fixed checks, not from a model. Checks that can misfire are labelled "heuristic". A model only explains a finding; it never adds, hides or closes one.

    6Fix a finding

    Lantern writes the instructions; your AI assistant makes the change.

    1. On a finding, click Fix this.
    2. Under "Paste this into your AI assistant", click Copy the prompt, then paste it into the assistant you build with. Or click Send to… and pick Claude Code, Cursor or ChatGPT.
    3. When the assistant has changed your code and you have saved it to your repository, go back to My runs and click Re-check. A fixed finding drops off the list.
    Good to knowIn the app on your own computer you can also accept a risk on purpose, with a written reason; the report keeps that reason next to the finding.

    7Follow a path through your app

    See how a click in the browser travels to your server and your data.

    1. In an open run, click Paths.
    2. Pick a view: Lanes (one row per part of the stack), Path, Flowchart or Graph.
    3. Type a name into Search functions to find one part of your code.
    4. On the Graph, drag to move and scroll or pinch to zoom; click Full screen or press F for more room.
    Good to knowEvery link on the map says how sure Lantern is about it. "Likely" links are good guesses backed by measured accuracy; calls Lantern could not work out are counted, not guessed.

    8Understand a function

    Each part of your code, explained four ways.

    1. On the map, click a function.
    2. Switch between the tabs: Technical (for an engineer), Everyday (for anyone), Where it sits (Mapped, in Advanced) and Scenario.
    3. For words written by a model, click Explain in plain words at the top of the run, read what will be sent, and click I agree.
    Good to knowBefore you ask for explanations, each function still shows lenses Lantern builds from its own map, and says so. Explanations use your free allowance; Lantern asks once before any code is sent to a model.

    9See if you're ready to ship

    One score out of 100 and a letter grade, from ten groups of checks.

    1. In an open run, click Ready to ship?.
    2. Read the headline under the grade: it names the next thing to do.
    3. Scroll through the groups (Security findings, Secrets and config, Tests, and more) to see which checks passed and which did not.
    4. Click What protects you to see protections your app has, should check, or should add.
    Good to knowA check that does not apply to your app is skipped and does not count against you.

    10Share your proof

    Give an engineer, a client or a teammate a read-only link.

    1. In an open run, click Share, read the warning, then click Create link.
    2. Click Copy and send the link. Anyone with it can open the report, its evidence page and its badge.
    3. To turn it off, open Share again and click Stop sharing. The old link stops working.
    4. For a file instead, click Report and then click Save as PDF.
    Good to knowSharing is off until you click Create link. Findings can include partly hidden keys and where your app is weakest, so share with people you trust or fix those first.

    11Ask Lantern

    A question about your check, or a word you don't know.

    1. Click the Ask Lantern button at the bottom right of any screen.
    2. Type your question and press Enter.
    3. Got an error on screen? Drop or paste a screenshot into the box and ask what went wrong.
    Good to knowLantern answers from its own results for your check and does not guess; if it names a finding that does not exist, the answer says "[unknown finding]" instead. It uses your free allowance.

    12Make Lantern yours

    Light or dark, simple or detailed, and what a check keeps.

    1. Click Settings at the top.
    2. Under Appearance, pick a Theme (match this computer, light or dark) and pick Simple or Advanced under How much to show.
    3. Under What a new check keeps, choose your default: map and findings only, or with code snippets.
    4. Under Account, click Sign out, or click Delete my account to remove your account and every run.
    Good to knowSimple shows plain sentences and one next step; Advanced shows every table and panel. Phones always use Simple. The moon and sun button at the top switches light and dark in one click.

    What on this page is not real

    • Every demo is an animation drawn by this page, not a screen recording. The screens are simplified copies of Lantern's real ones; some buttons and details are left out to keep them readable.
    • The account "mira-sample", the app "notes-app", its link, findings, scores and answers are made up for the demos.
    • "Connect private repositories" appears only when the site has GitHub connection switched on.
    • Explanations and Ask Lantern need a model and use your free allowance; when the allowance is used up they stop until it resets.
